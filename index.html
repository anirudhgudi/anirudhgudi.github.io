Robotics Engineer, Mechanical Design Roots

I started in mechanical design — three years shaping Body-in-White sheet-metal structures for Honda at L&T Technology Services — before moving into robotics for my master's at UC Riverside. That order matters: I think about a robot as physical hardware first, tolerances, torque, wire routing, before I think about the control stack running on top of it.

Lately that's meant pairing a classical footstep planner with a residual reinforcement-learning policy for quadruped locomotion, tuning ArduPilot flight stacks on drone and lighter-than-air platforms at Worldwide Aeros, and building an 8-DOF snake robot from scratch — servo selection, 3D-printed links, and every wire in between. I like the projects where the mechanical design and the software have to agree with each other, because that's usually where the interesting problems live.

Beyond the Bench

Where I'm spending curiosity right now, past what's on the resume.

Robot Learning

My capstone paired a classical Raibert footstep planner with a PPO residual policy — hand-designed control isn't going away, but it's a solid scaffold for a learned correction to sit on top of. I'm interested in where that boundary should actually sit, especially for contact-rich locomotion and manipulation, and how far it can push before hand-tuning stops paying off.

Perception

State estimation sits right next to my mechanical background — sensor placement and mounting are as much a design problem as a filtering one. I've built EKF/UKF fusion pipelines combining IMU, GPS, and encoder data; going deeper into vision- and LiDAR-based perception for legged and aerial platforms is the natural next step.
